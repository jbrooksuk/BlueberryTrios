## Design Context

### Users
Logic-puzzle enthusiasts — the sudoku / nonogram / Picross crowd. They care about technique, difficulty progression, and the *integrity* of the puzzle. They come to Berroku looking for a deeper, quieter challenge and will stay for craft, not gimmicks. They notice small details: how a grid is ruled, how a hint is phrased, how a streak is celebrated without condescension.

They are adults, often opinionated about their tools, and likely arrive via word-of-mouth or puzzle communities rather than ads. The marketing site and the app both need to feel like they respect the player's intelligence.

### Brand Personality
**Calm, tactile, considered.** A little dry, a little warm, never loud. The voice is the voice of a thoughtful puzzle editor — confident, curious, happy to explain the rules once and then get out of the way.

Emotional goals: composure, satisfaction, the quiet pride of solving something hard. Never hype, never urgency, never "LEVEL UP!!" energy. Delight should arrive at the moment of solving, not from the surrounding chrome.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Canonical theme**: dark navy. Keep the current moody canvas — `#0b1628` family — because that's where the berry blue reads richest and the puzzle lives most comfortably.
- **Palette**: retain the existing berry blue (`#3584E4`) and leaf green hues, but refine their values for a more tactile, less saturated feel. Think ink and botanical, not neon. Tint neutrals toward the brand blue rather than using flat grays. Use OKLCH and reduce chroma at the extremes.
- **References (positive)**:
  - *NYT Games* — editorial restraint, serif headlines, respect for the puzzle, generous whitespace, quiet confidence.
  - *Panic (Playdate, app marketing)* — tactile craft, personality in microcopy, playful without being childish, materials that feel made rather than rendered.
- **Anti-references (explicitly NOT this)**:
  - Tech-startup dark mode — no cyan-on-black, no neon glow, no "futuristic" gradient halos, no crypto/AI aesthetic.
  - Kids' educational app — no cartoon mascots, no primary-color cheer, no rounded mascot-with-icon-above-every-heading patterns.
  - Generic AI landing page — no glassmorphism cards, no gradient text, no border-left accent stripes, no hero metric boxes, no "three identical feature cards in a row" layouts.
- **Typography direction**: the current Inter + Fraunces pairing is a reflex default (both on the impeccable ban list). When redesigning, reach for something more distinctive — a display face with editorial character (not Fraunces / Cormorant / Playfair) paired with a body face that has warmth and slight personality (not Inter / DM Sans / Plus Jakarta). Favor type that feels like it could be set in a puzzle book, not a SaaS dashboard.
- **Accessibility floor**: WCAG AA. 4.5:1 body contrast, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, keyboard-navigable, sensible focus states. Dark-on-dark is easy to mis-tune — light text on the navy canvas needs slightly loosened line-height to compensate for the optical lightening.

### Design Principles
1. **Respect the puzzle.** The grid is the hero; chrome recedes. If a decorative element isn't earning its place next to the puzzle, cut it.
2. **Tactile over digital.** Reach for materials, weight, and paper-grain texture before reaching for glassmorphism, glow, or gradient. If it could live on a printed puzzle page, that's a good sign.
3. **Editorial craft.** Composition, typography, and rhythm do the heavy lifting. Left-aligned, asymmetric, confidently spaced — not centered feature grids.
4. **Rare, purposeful color.** Berry blue and leaf green are accents, not wallpaper. The dark navy canvas is doing most of the work; color appears when it means something (the berry itself, a solved state, a key CTA).
5. **Quiet confidence in voice.** Microcopy has personality but earns its place. No exclamation points, no hype, no redundant restatement of headings. Say it once, say it well, trust the reader.
